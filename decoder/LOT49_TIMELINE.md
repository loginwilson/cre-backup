## THE RECORDING BATCH IS THE TRANSACTION

The single most useful structural fact on this parcel. A refinancing does not
produce a mortgage; it produces a **stereotyped set of five instruments filed
under one 13-digit batch id**:

| instrument | what it does | taxed? |
|---|---|---|
| **TL&R** | terminates the OLD assignment of leases and rents | no |
| **ASST** | assigns the OLD mortgage to the new lender | no |
| **MTGE** | the NEW money | **YES — this is the only real borrowing** |
| **AGMT** | consolidates old + new into one lien (exemption 255) | no |
| **AL&R** | re-pledges leases and rents to the new lender | no |

⚠ **Summing the face amounts in a batch double- and triple-counts.** The 2023
batch shows $265,490,000 of "face" across its documents. **The actual new money
was $25,490,000.** AGMT and AL&R mirror each other; ASST and TL&R carry nothing.

## WHAT THE PARCEL IS, IN FOUR SENTENCES

A Long Island family held a Chelsea site with a $226,000 savings-bank mortgage
until 2007, when they sold to **Extell for $42.7M**. Extell spent **$12.4M
buying 53,578+ sf of unused floor area from four neighbours** — two of them
residential co-op boards on West 24th Street — assembling lots 21, 22, 23, 53,
55 and 56 into one zoning lot, then **sold to the Lam Group in 2013 for $67.5M
without building anything (~$12.4M gross)**. Lam built the 40-storey hotel in
2016 on construction debt of $65.7M from Goldman Sachs.
Today it carries a **$123,000,000** consolidated position whose lineage runs
back to a **1990** mortgage, and the true new money raised across 2023 and 2025
was **$28.49M**, not the $514M the face amounts suggest.
