# Candidate unnamed functions

The eleven functions are a taxonomy someone wrote. **If a category of real-world
event fits none of them, no checker finds it** — every reader works from the same
list, so cross-checking cannot reach this class. It is the one failure no arrangement
of models catches.

So v3 says: *if something on the page fits none of them, that is a finding — write it
down rather than forcing it.* This file is where those go.

**A candidate is not a function.** It is promoted only when it recurs across
documents and a reader shows that filing it under an existing function loses
something a consumer needs. One document is a curiosity; three is a taxonomy gap.

---

## 1. REGISTRY ACT — *what did the registry do?*

**Seen: RC_1598772. Reported independently by three readers.**

Rule 1 sends the recording *date* to the brief and silently takes three other facts
with it:

- **the time** — *"Recorded April 25, 1911 at 9 a.m."* Time fixes same-day recording
  priority, which decides which of two instruments filed on one day wins.
- **the registry's own act** — an event performed by the clerk, on the *instrument*,
  not by a party on the parcel
- **the return-to party** — *"C. Livingston Bostwick, for Wood, Harmon & Co.,
  Broadway, N.Y. City"*, the only appearance in the whole document of the grantor's
  agent and address

None of the eleven asks *what did the registry do?* or *who gets the paper back?*

**Why it is not just a missing field:** every existing function asks a question about
**the parcel**. This asks about **the instrument**. That is a different subject, which
is what makes it a function candidate rather than a column.

**Sharpened by:** rules 1 and 5 of v3 contradict each other here. Rule 5 names the
return-to party as worth catching; rule 1, obeyed literally, deletes the row that
would hold it. An early round already lost party addresses as an undetected error —
this is the same hole one page over.

**Also on this document:** the endorsement carried the fee on other documents in the
corpus (`FT_1000000027200` $16.00 unlabelled; `RC_300106` $59.00). Two readers checked
both margins at 900 dpi here and found no fee, tax or revenue stamp — *"I found
nothing"*, not *"the document says there is nothing."*

## 2. PRIVATE APPROVAL RIGHT — *who holds a discretionary veto?*

**Seen: RC_1598772. One reader.**

> *no fence may be built unless its "nature, kind, shape and material" is first made
> known to an officer of the company and has received his sanction and approval **in
> writing***

and, folded into the reserved rights:

> *no such building shall be erected unless the plans and specifications for same
> shall have been first submitted to and approved by said party of the first part*

**`PERMIT` is explicitly government-only.** The reader filed both under `ENVELOPE`,
which captures the *constraint* and loses the fact that **a named private party holds
a discretionary veto, with no stated standard and no appeal.**

That is the part a title examiner needs: not "there is a fence rule" but "a company
that may no longer exist must approve your fence in writing."

**Expect this to recur.** Developer covenant schemes, condominium declarations and
homeowners' associations are built out of exactly this shape.

## 3. PARCEL DEFINITION — *what reference frame makes this description mean anything?*

**Seen: RC_1598772. One reader.**

> *on a certain plan of lots called South New York, Addition Number Four, surveyed for
> Wood Harmon & Co., 1906, by Lewis T. Haney, Civil Engineer and City Surveyor, and
> filed **or intended to be filed** in the Clerk's Office of Richmond County … July 5,
> 1907, as map no. 995 B*

**Map 995 B is what makes "lot 16, block 403" mean anything at all.** Without the
filed map, the lot numbers are not a location.

The reader filed it under `IDENTITY`, whose triggers are all about persons and
entities — aliases, name changes, successors. A map is not a party.

**Two things are lost:** that this is a *definition* the parcel depends on, and the
document's own hedge — *"filed **or intended to be filed**"* — which asserts a date
and a number while declining to confirm the act happened. Rule 7 distinguishes *"I
found nothing"* from *"the document says there is nothing"* and has no third state for
**"the document declines to say."**

**Note:** 1907-07-05 is the date a broken checker once mistook for the recording date.
It is neither instrument, acknowledgment, nor recording — a fourth kind of date with
no role in the labelled block.

## Watch list — not yet seen, predicted by the gaps above

- **tax liens and mechanic's liens** — `ENCUMBRANCE` covers the burden; nothing asks
  who the lienor is or what statute created it
- **party wall agreements** — one appeared inside a surveyor's boundary course on
  `2002122700120002` and was found only because the sweep asks all eleven
- **easements running to a utility** — burden on this parcel, benefit to no parcel
- **death, probate, and involuntary transfer** — `TITLE` assumes a grantor acts

## How to use this file

Readers: when something fits none of the eleven, **do not force it**. Say so under an
explicit heading and describe what would be lost by filing it under the nearest
function. One reader did exactly that this round and it is why two of these entries
exist.

Judge: promote a candidate when it appears on a **third** document, or when a reader
shows a consumer decision that the existing filing gets wrong.
