### The near-miss, found independently by two readers

m1's measured spec recorded `TERMINATE: 0` **with a justification**: *"and it should
be 0 — `until` is the mechanism for a self-expiring scheme."*

> *"That is correct about covenant schemes and exactly wrong as a general rule. A
> satisfaction of mortgage is a `TERMINATE` and almost nothing else. **The mode was
> one round away from being written off on single-class evidence.**"*

And the distinction, stated precisely:

> *"**`until` handles a state that expires by its own terms; `TERMINATE` handles a
> state ended by a later instrument.** m1 had no later instrument, so it could not
> distinguish them."*

**This is the entire justification for cross-class probing, demonstrated.** A
measurement taken across five readers and 99 rows was about to become a rule, and it
was a fact about one document class wearing the clothes of a general law.

`VALUE` failed the same way in the other direction: unanimous on m1, zero here. A
satisfaction recites no consideration.

## The structural finding — 3 of 3, and it is large

**The eleven functions all ask a question about a *parcel*. Instruments routinely act
on things that are not parcels.**

> *"A function for instrument-directed acts would cover satisfactions, assignments,
> subordinations, modifications and consolidations — **most of what a registry
> actually holds.**"*

Concretely, a `TERMINATE` row cannot say **what it terminates**:

> *"E1 and E2 end a mortgage recorded 2001-01-12 at reel 3221 page 495. `until` puts
> an end date on this row's own state; there is no machine field saying *this event
> closes the state opened by that instrument*. Resolve has to parse the pointer out of
> `terms` prose — exactly the 'faithful but not consumable' failure the framework
> names, sitting on the single most important fact a satisfaction carries."*

**The checker already proves the pointer is parseable**: `REF 5 corpus pointer(s):
reel 3221 p495`. It is reported as a note and no column carries it.

One reader connected it to m2's Lien Law §13 trust — an obligation binding a *fund*,
not land — and called it *"the strongest structural signal I have produced across
three documents."* Two classes, two clause types, one gap.

## 🆕 A new candidate function, with cross-century corroboration: `CONSTRUCTION`

**A rule the document gives for reading itself.**

| document | clause |
|---|---|
| 2003 satisfaction | *"The information on this page will control for indexing purposes in the event of any conflict with the rest of the document."* |
| 1955 deed (m2) | *"The word 'party' shall be construed as if it read 'parties' whenever the sense of this indenture so requires."* |

Both change the meaning of every other row without asserting anything about a parcel.
Both do real work — the first governs a **live conflict on its own face**, the second
is what makes singular printed form language reach two grantors and two grantees.
Filing either under `IDENTITY` claims the document asserts who someone *is*; it does
not. **It governs rather than states.**

Reached independently by all three readers; named by one.

⚠ **Two instances, two classes, 48 years apart. The promotion bar is a third
document** — it is not met, and it is the strongest candidate yet because, in one
reader's words, *"unlike the other two candidates it is not a symptom of one era."*

**And one existing candidate should be retired, not promoted:** the parcel-definition
(filed map) candidate did not fire, *"and the reason is informative — a modern
instrument carries the tax BBL directly instead of pointing at a filed map, so that
candidate is a property of pre-BBL conveyancing, not a gap in the function set."*

## Card 9 is not wrong. It is pre-digital. — 3 of 3

Card 9 says *check the index, trust neither side, correct nothing.* **That assumes two
independent witnesses.**

> *"On this document the cover page **is** the index: the presenter typed it, the
> register indexed from it, and rd is derived from it. rd and the cover page are not
> two records that agree — **they are one record copied twice.** The genuinely
> independent witness is p2, the instrument."*

**Agreement between rd and the cover page earns no credit at all.** And the conflict is
live: the cover names `CHASE MANHATTAN MORTGAGE`, the instrument names `THE CHASE
MANHATTAN BANK`, and it is executed by `JPMORGAN CHASE BANK F/K/A THE CHASE MANHATTAN
BANK` — three names, a link stated between only two, and the question is **who had
standing to discharge.**

Worse: **the document states a tie-break rule and the schema has nowhere to record
that one exists**, let alone which way it points.

**Testable prediction, recorded before the next document:** every born-digital

**Card 5's third state** — *the document declines* — has fired on exactly one document

### Two cards fired in ways their wording did not anticipate

**Card 4 inverts on digital documents.** On m1 and m2 the pressure was *illegibility*.
Here everything is machine-printed and perfectly legible, and the danger moved to
**fields that are legible and wrong**: `Property Type: DWELLING ONLY - 3 FAMILY` sits
in the same panel as a unit-numbered condominium lot on the eleventh floor. *"Card 4
guards only against writing what you cannot point at. It has nothing to say about a
value you can point at that the document itself refutes."*

**Card 7's premise is wrong and its conclusion is right.** It says the page you cannot
read easily carries the fee, the return-to party and the parcel identification. Here
the *trivially readable* cover page carries all three and half the rows. **Rewrite the
premise as "the page that is not the instrument."**

**Card 8 fired hardest of any card, on a class it was not written from:** four page
counts, all correct about different things — `Document Page Count: 1`, `PAGE 1 OF 2`,
rd `"pages": "2"`, MANIFEST `2`. **Fully general, confirmed.**
