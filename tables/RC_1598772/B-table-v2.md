## Brief

On 14 April 1911 Wood Harmon Richmond Realty Company, a New York corporation,
granted and released lots 16 and 17 in Block 403 — a 40 by 100 foot parcel on the
west side of Heberton Avenue, 214.28 feet north of Caswell Avenue, in the Borough
of Richmond — to Minnie A. Sweeney of 409 Third Street, Brooklyn, for one dollar
and other valuable considerations of unstated amount. The estate is a fee, but the
operative words are "grant and release" of "all its right, title and interest",
with no warranty anywhere in the instrument; the printed clause subjecting the
grant to assessment liens was struck out.

The weight of the deed is a builder's covenant Sweeney gives back, binding her own
lot and nothing else: detached or semi-detached dwelling only, no more than two
families, two storeys with a cellar and no flat roof, at least $2,000 as a
one-family house or $3,000 as a double, 15 feet back from Heberton Avenue with any
outbuilding 60 feet back, one house and one outbuilding per 20 by 100 foot unit,
no fence without the company's written approval, no liquor sold, no trade or
business at all, and a long list of named nuisance trades excluded. The company
exempts its own land in South New York, Addition Number Four from the density and
building-type limits, and reserves the use of lots on four named ways. All of it
expires by its own terms on 1 January 1915, three years and eight months after the
deed, stated once in one printed sentence on page 2.

Leonidas Keever, Vice-President, acknowledged the deed before Elizabeth Roth,
Commissioner of Deeds, on 18 April 1911; it was recorded 25 April 1911 at 9 a.m.
in book 396, page 1, and directed back to C. Livingston Bostwick for Wood, Harmon
& Co. rd carries no parties and an amount of $0.00 against a deed reciting one
dollar; its two BBLs are the only registry field that checks against the document,
and they agree.
